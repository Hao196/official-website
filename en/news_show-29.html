Stranding Machine Process Flow and Common Troubleshooting Methods

For stranding on a stranding machine, if it is concentric layer stranding, the first thing to consider is whether the number and size of the pay-off reels of the stranding machine are suitable for it; the second is the direction of lay, the lay length, and whether the wires need to be untwisted. For a multi-wire stranded conductor, the deformation of the stranded wire must also be considered. The strand structure, specification and material should be analyzed and the process data determined before finally selecting a suitable stranding machine. For buncher products, besides the number and size of the pay-off reels, determining the size of the take-up reel is far more important than it is for stranding machines.

If there is no strict requirement on how large the pay-off reel for a coarse single wire should be, order according to the specified length - fine wires use small reels and coarse wires use large reels.

Generally speaking, the speed of a cage-type twisting machine is far lower than that of a fork-type stranding machine, and the speed of a fork-type stranding machine is far lower than that of a tubular-type twisting machine. Another point is that the common twin-twist machine takes the form of a floating double-twist cradle.

How to do a good job of self-inspection of the stranded output?

For an enterprise, the normal operation of the machine is very necessary. To make the finished products look better, deliver better quality, and minimize losses caused by human factors, the following work should be done well while the stranding machine is in use:

1. Check that the stranded conductor from the preceding process is not reddened or blackened;

2. Confirm that the wire count and direction from the preceding process are correct, the marking is complete and the connections are neat;

3. Verify that the packaging is intact and that the marking and specification meet the process requirements;

4. Check whether the actual production operation complies with the requirements of the process card;

5. Check that the stranded wire is round in appearance, well compacted and well formed, with no missing wires, jumping wires or missing wrapping;

6. Check that the loading of this process meets the loading requirements, the reel is intact, and the take-up winding is neat.

Causes of wrong stranding direction and disordered cable arrangement on the stranding machine, and how to eliminate them. During use, due to improper operation or other natural factors, the stranding machine may produce wrong stranding direction, unqualified lay length, disordered take-up winding, or wire compression.

Wrong lay direction and unqualified lay length (mainly too large) are mainly caused by judgment errors. Operators should strengthen their sense of responsibility during operation, work strictly in accordance with the process card, and step up in-process inspection to prevent the production of human-caused rejects and waste. If the take-up arrangement is disordered with wire compression, it is basically caused by the following: stranded products are generally supplied on reels. Large cross-sections should be arranged one by one, and in particular the bottom layers must be arranged neatly. When the wire is traversed or the direction is reversed, because of the one-time stop, pay special attention to ensuring that there is no excessive gap between the upper and lower layers; the lay spacing should be appropriate, otherwise wire compression is likely to occur.

To deal with the above situations, the following measures can be taken:

First, adjust the take-up tension well. When laying out cables manually, it is recommended to arrange them in a flexible manner. Second, the spacing of the cable arrangement should vary according to the diameter of the core wires. Laying out the first layer is the key, and it must be arranged neatly. For the following layers, manual wiring is needed when adjusting each layer to ensure the wiring quality. Third, strengthen the sense of operational responsibility, observe the cable laying quality at any time, and adjust promptly whenever problems are found. Also pay attention to securing the two wire ends and the edge spacing.

The stranding principle and the definition of lay length of the stranding machine:

The stranding machine is a wire and cable machine used for stranding core wires such as power cords, earphone wires and electronic wires. The main stranding principle is: when the copper wires pass through the stranding bow of the stranding machine, the bow causes each individual copper wire to be wrapped helically through circular motion. Copper wires of different specifications and counts are twisted together in a certain arrangement order and at a certain lay, forming a conductor of larger diameter. This stranded conductor is much softer than a single copper wire of the same diameter, and the wires made from it also have better bending performance.

Definition of the lay of the stranding machine. Each individual wire appears once at a fixed distance along the stranding direction; this fixed distance is the lay length of the strand (pitch for short). Another definition of lay: it refers to the vertical distance that the stranded wire advances after being rotated 360 degrees around the stranding axis.

A bunched wire is formed by twisting multiple single wires together. Generally, the individual wires in a bunched conductor are many and fine, which increases the flexibility of the wire and cable and improves the reliability of the circuit connection. To achieve better flexibility or higher reliability, the conductors of some wires and cables are twisted. To bunch or twist the single wires at a certain helix angle (or lay), the machine must meet the following requirements: first, all the single wires should rotate around the central axis of the machine; second, the twisted product should advance in a straight line. By changing the coordination of these two motions, the helix angle can be adjusted so that the stranded wire or bunched wire produced meets the structural requirements.

The stranding machine consists of the pay-off unit, the haul-off unit, the traverse and take-up unit, the drive and transmission system, and the control system. In addition, there are devices such as separating plates, combining dies (also called pressing dies) and length counters.

The stranding machine produces stranded wire of relatively large specification. When stranding, apart from the central single wire, the pay-off reels of the remaining single wires are all placed in the pay-off section (such as the cage). Through its rotation, the single wires form a stranded layer around the central wire. According to the number of stranded layers and the number of single wires in each layer, stranding machines generally have several (or several sections of) pay-off parts that rotate separately, so that stranded wire with a different lay direction in each layer can be produced; it is therefore especially suitable for producing concentric layer stranded wire. The bunched wire produced by a bunching machine is of small specification. It is formed by the rotation of the basket frame or rotating body in the take-up section. Because all the running actions of the bunching machine take place in the take-up section, with the change gear mechanism installed inside the rotating basket frame where space is limited, the bunching machine can only produce twisted products of one lay direction and smaller specification. However, the number of single wires to be bunched can be flexibly configured as required.

What is a pay-off stand? How many types of pay-off stands are there?

The pay-off stand is a widely used auxiliary machine in the wire and cable industry, and it performs the pay-off function for the stranding machine.

Generally, according to power transmission, pay-off stands can be divided into:

active pay-off stands and passive pay-off stands. By style, pay-off stands can be divided into vertical pay-off stands and horizontal pay-off stands. By axis mode, they can be divided into axial pay-off stands and shaftless pay-off stands.

Common pay-off stands include active pay-off stands, vertical power pay-off stands, twin-shaft power pay-off stands, twin-shaft multi-head tray-type active pay-off stands, shaftless pay-off stands, shaftless take-up stands, passive pay-off stands, and others.
