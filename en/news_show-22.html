Introduction to the Operating Principle of Stranding Machines

Stranding machines have a very wide range of applications. Apart from the standard stranding machine, automatic and fully automatic stranding machines can all wind coils of different sizes, such as toroidal (ring) coils and square coils — the two most common types. These types differ mainly in their winding settings: every type of coil must be set with the appropriate basic parameters, and the tension, wire-guiding and other techniques must also be adjusted in good time, so that the wound coils meet the process requirements and achieve the distinctive appearance required.

When winding fine coils, a stranding machine generally uses one of two traverse methods: free (random) layering and forced (positive) layering. Each method has its own advantages. Free layering relies on the surface tension of the wire and the oscillation of the guide wheel or pendulum to lay the wire. Since the guide rollers are set some distance away from the coil structure, with the right adjustment every turn can be laid tightly against the previous one, so that the finished coil can achieve the desired mirror-glass effect. However, adjustment is very difficult, mainly because of the large number of control variables on the machine. Forced layering uses the coordinated motion of the machine spindle and the traverse mechanism, so that for each revolution of the coil the traverse moves forward by a fixed distance, usually one wire diameter per turn. With today's CNC technology this is not hard to implement: once the winding parameters have been set in advance, winding can be carried out without much adjustment. However, sustained testing has shown that the forced layering method is very difficult to use when rapidly winding coils with wire of less than 0.1 mm, and scrambled winding frequently occurs.

A Dongguan stranding machine manufacturer must first analyse the situation and determine which category a problem belongs to. Taking a traverse-type stranding machine as an example, several common problems are described below.

1. Untidy wire layering. Analyse the specific situation to determine whether the problem occurs during normal production or after a product change and the subsequent adjustment. The former is generally a system configuration problem, while the latter is usually a settings problem. Configuration problems are typically caused by loose clamping fixtures, loose fixing screws on the traverse mechanism, or poor lubrication of the traverse mechanism, which overloads the AC servomotor and causes it to lose steps, resulting in untidy layering.

2. Inaccurate stopping position of the machine spindle. This is usually caused by a fault in the brake. For example, the brake pads may be loose or worn out. It is also related to the brake time setting: if the brake time is too short, the spindle will not stop at the required position. The brake pads must be replaced, or the brake timing adjusted.

3. Inaccurate coil-turn counting. If an inaccurate count occurs in a traverse-type stranding machine, it is generally caused by a configuration problem with the sensor or the controller. When this happens, the stranding machine cannot operate, and the Dongguan stranding machine manufacturer should generally be called in to repair it.
