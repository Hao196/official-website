Stranding Principle and Lay Length Definition



The stranding machine is a cable machine used for stranding core wires such as power cords, earphone wires and electronic wires. Its main stranding principle is: when the copper wires pass through the stranding bow on the stranding machine, the stranding bow, through circular motion, makes each individual copper wire wind helically together; stranded copper wire is the field that uses the largest quantity of single copper wire. Copper wires of different specifications and counts are twisted together in a certain arrangement order and at a certain lay, becoming a conductor of larger diameter. This stranded conductor is much softer than a single copper wire of the same diameter, and the wires made from it also have better bending performance.

Definition of the lay of the stranding machine: each individual wire appears once at a fixed distance along the stranding direction; this fixed distance is the lay length of the strand, or pitch for short. Another definition of lay: it refers to the vertical distance that the stranded wire body advances after rotating 360 degrees about the stranding axis, in mm; in English it is called the pitch.

A bunched wire is formed by twisting multiple single wires. Generally, the single wires of a bunch are many and fine, which increases the flexibility of the wire and cable and also improves the reliability of circuit connection. To obtain better flexibility or high reliability, the conductors of some wires and cables adopt the twisted form.

To bunch or strand the single wires at a certain helix angle (or lay), the equipment must meet the following requirements: first, all the single wires should make a rotational motion around the central axis of the equipment; second, the stranded product should make a straight-line forward motion. By changing the coordination of these two motions, the size of the helix angle can be adjusted so that the stranded wire or bunched wire produced meets the structural requirements.

The stranding machine is mainly composed of the pay-off unit, the haul-off unit, the traverse and take-up unit, the drive and transmission system, and the control system. In addition, there are devices such as separating plates, combining dies (also called pressing dies), and length counters.

The stranding machine produces stranded wire of relatively large specification. When stranding, apart from the central single wire, the pay-off reels of the other single wires are all placed in the pay-off part (such as the stranding cage). Through its rotation, the single wires form a stranded layer around the central wire. According to the number of stranded layers and the number of single wires in each layer, stranding machines generally have several (or several sections of) separately rotating pay-off parts, so that stranded wire with a different lay direction in each layer can be made; hence it is especially suitable for producing concentric lay stranded wire. The bunched wire produced by a bunching machine is of smaller specification; it is formed by the rotation of the basket frame or the rotating body in the take-up part. Because the running actions of the bunching machine all take place in the take-up part, with the speed change mechanism installed inside the rotating basket frame where the space is limited, the bunching machine can only make twisted products of one lay direction and smaller specification. However, the number of single wires to be bunched can be flexibly configured as needed.
