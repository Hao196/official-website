Work Instructions for Extruder Machine Operators

An extruder production line operator is the person primarily responsible for maintaining the equipment and prolonging the service life of the extruder. Every operator should understand that reducing the frequency of repairs and parts replacement and extending the working life of the extruder is an important technical measure for lowering the production management costs of plastic products and improving overall economic efficiency. Keeping the equipment operating at its best over the long term, with stable plastic quality and output, is the basic condition that ensures safe production and sound profitability.

When operators follow the operating procedures conscientiously and take full responsibility for their work, that in itself is the best maintenance and care the equipment can receive. New workers must therefore learn and memorise the extruder operating procedures before going on duty, and may operate the equipment independently only after passing an actual hands-on operating examination.

To work on your own and become a qualified extruder operator, you should pay attention to the following items.

(1) Shift handover
Any problems encountered with the equipment during the shift must be clearly communicated to the next shift. Record the production output, product quality and equipment status in the shift handover log.

(2) At the start of the shift
After completing the handover procedures, first check the production quality of the products, verify the quality of the raw materials, and check the amount of material in the extruder hopper. Report any problems to the supervisor without delay.

(3) Check whether the barrel and the process control temperatures of the extruder are within the process temperature ranges required for production. Depending on the product quality, appropriate adjustments may be made based on your own operating experience.

(4) Routine inspection
Walk a full circuit around the equipment and check the condition of the drive components while it is running: observe whether the rotation is smooth and normal, listen for any abnormal operating sounds, and check whether the temperatures of the heated zones are normal.

(5) Check the gauges: on the operating control box, read the main motor voltage and current. The ammeter pointer should be within the normal rated current range.

(6) When production is running normally, the extruder screw rotates steadily and the plastic product is extruded smoothly and uniformly from the die head. At intervals, inspect the appearance of the product, or take samples and test whether all its parameters meet the requirements.

(7) In the event of a sudden power failure during operation, immediately switch off the main motor, the heating and the material feed system, and return all speed-adjustment controls to zero. When power is restored, first reheat the barrel to the required process temperature and hold it there for a period of time. Turn the screw by hand through the motor and belt connection to gauge the resistance. If it turns relatively easily, start the motor at low speed and resume work. If the ammeter pointer exceeds the rated current, or the screw speed is not steady on starting, stop the line promptly, investigate the cause (the material in the barrel may not have reached temperature), identify and rectify the fault, and only then restart the machine.

(8) If the machine is to be shut down for a long period, no cleaning is needed if the material remaining in the barrel is a polyolefin type. However, if the barrel contains nylon/polyamide or similar materials, the barrel must be cleared of the remaining material. The screw should then be removed and cleaned, together with the barrel, removing all residue from the screw and the barrel. After cleaning, coat the screw with a layer of anti-rust oil, wrap it well and hang it vertically in a dry, well-ventilated place.
