What Is the Difference Between a Cage Strander and a Rigid Frame Strander

Cage-type and frame-type stranding machines are constantly compared with each other. Today, let's take a look at the differences between the two.

I. Production speed
Frame-type strander: because it adopts a frame structure, the central shaft is not loaded by the weight of the reels, so the machine has high rigidity. It offers good overall performance, a small turning radius and a high production speed.
Cage-type strander: its small turning radius and the complex structure of its de-twisting device limit the production speed of the cage-type strander.

II. De-twisting capability
Frame-type strander: the frame-type strander cannot de-twist. During stranding, the individual wires acquire torsional stress, so that high-toughness hard-drawn round conductors are not laid tightly and easily spread apart. For this reason, some frame-type stranders are fitted with a pre-forming device ahead of the stranding cage to eliminate the torsional stress in the individual wires.
Cage-type strander: it carries out de-twisting stranding by means of a de-twisting ring, chain or sprocket arrangements and planetary gear de-twisting mechanisms, making it suitable for individual elements that cannot withstand torque, such as optical fibres. Cage-type stranders must be used for high-toughness round wires (such as aluminium-clad steel wires) and for rope-lay stranded cores.

III. Stranding of insulated cable cores
Frame-type strander: because it cannot perform de-twisting stranding, it can only be used for stranding solid-conductor insulated cores of small sizes.
Cage-type strander: it can perform stranding with or without de-twisting and is suitable for all kinds of insulated cable cores.

IV. Tension control
Frame-type strander: tension control is achieved by mechanical friction or pneumatic methods.
