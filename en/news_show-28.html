Common Problems in Extruder Production and How to Handle Them

1. Problems encountered by the extrusion machine during production:

Plastic scorching. Plastic scorching is a common quality defect in the plastic extrusion process. Its main symptoms are: an excessively high temperature reading; heavy smoke and a strong, pungent odor at the die, with crackling or popping in serious cases; scorched particles in the extruded plastic layer; and continuous bubbles at the weld seam. The main causes are:

- The temperature control is set too high, reaching the thermal degradation temperature of the plastic.
- The screw has not been cleaned for a long time, so the scorched material that has built up is forced out together with the molten plastic.
- The heating or stopping time is too long, so the plastic remains in the barrel for too long and degrades from the heat.
- The temperature control instrument fails or is inaccurate, causing high-temperature degradation.

The cooling system of the extrusion machine is not switched on, causing the material to overheat from shear friction. During extrusion, therefore, you should step up checks to confirm that the heating and cooling systems are operating properly; allow the extrusion temperatures to be set according to the process requirements and the screw speed; reasonably control the heating time; and clean the extrusion system on a regular basis.

Poor plasticization of the extrudate. The plasticization issue was mentioned earlier in the discussion of temperature control requirements. Poor plasticization generally appears as: a toad-skin texture on the extruded layer; a dull surface without gloss; and fine cracks.

2. How to handle faults when the extrusion machine is in production

The extrudate temperature is too low; too much filling compound overflows; the production line speed is too high, causing the sheath to be cooled too rapidly; the hot water trough temperature is too low and the trough is positioned too close to the die; the draw ratio of the die set is too small, or the die set is unreasonable, causing a loose sheath; and the melting point of the composite film of the longitudinally applied tape is too high. Based on the above causes, pay attention to the die configuration requirements and, where necessary, make adjustments according to the cable core. Do not let the sheath cool down too rapidly, so as to improve the bonding strength; do not lower the die head temperature excessively in an attempt to improve the sheath's dimensional stability; and control the filling amount of the compound. Ideally, when you lightly touch the cable core with a finger, a thin layer of compound can be scraped off.

The appearance of the extruded sheath fails to meet the standard. This appears specifically as scratches on the surface of the extruded layer; roughness with pinholes or micro-cracks; or any defect caused by poor plasticization. The main causes are: improper selection of the extrusion die sleeve. If it is too small, a rough, rippled surface results; if it is too large, gaps or loose extrusion occur; and if foreign matter is stuck in the die sleeve, it scratches the sheath surface.

If the die head heating temperature is too high, the surface becomes rough; if it is too low, micro-cracks appear. Damage in the sizing zone of the die sleeve, or the sheath being scratched inside the water trough, can also cause defects. For the above causes, the defects can be eliminated or prevented during production. In actual production, besides the defects and causes mentioned above, there are also problems such as take-up winding issues, longitudinal wrapping overlap width problems, and bamboo-joint-shaped appearance. If we observe and summarize more during production and grasp the main, essential causes of the problems, they will be solved and the quality of the cable can be improved further.
