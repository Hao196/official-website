How to Design Wire That Meets Flexing and Bending Test Requirements

■Conductor Structure Design (Very Important)

Conductors are normally built from a single strand or multiple strands, and are classified by material as bare copper, tinned copper, silver-plated copper, copper foil yarn, copper-clad steel and the like. The finer and softer the conductor, the better its flexing performance.

To improve flex life, conductors are usually built up from multiple strands, with fillers such as nylon yarn or Kevlar (ballistic yarn) added where necessary. For stranded conductors, the shorter the lay length, the better the flexing performance.

■Insulation Structure Design (Important)

Insulation protects and insulates the conductor. Insulation materials include FEP, PTFE, nylon, shrink-resistant PP, the PE family (low-density LDPE, medium-density MDPE and high-density HDPE), semi-rigid SR-PVC, PVC, Foam PE (not recommended for cables to be subjected to flexing tests) and Foam PE + skin (also not recommended for flexing-test cables), among others.

In terms of insulation materials, and with all other conditions being equal, the more cost-effective choices are FEP or PTFE insulation, followed by Hytrel, then nylon, then shrink-resistant PP (not ordinary PP), and then the PE family. Where keeping costs to a minimum is the deciding factor, the PVC series is the choice.

If the insulation wall is too thin, the conductor is not adequately protected; if it is too thick, the cable becomes too stiff. For video-signal cables such as DP (DisplayPort) and HDMI, the internal foamed signal conductors tend to break during flexing because foamed materials have poor flex resistance. For such cables, flexing performance is mainly improved through the conductor lay length, the construction and the filling of the stranded bundle.

■Filling/Spacing Structure Design (General)

Filling serves two purposes: it maintains the roundness of the cable, and it protects the cable from damage during bending, thereby contributing to flex resistance. Common fillers include nylon yarn (ballistic yarn), Kevlar, cotton yarn, PP jute-fibre filler, hemp rope, PVC filler rods and the like.

In order of flex resistance, Kevlar out-performs nylon yarn (ballistic yarn), nylon yarn out-performs cotton yarn, cotton yarn out-performs PP jute-fibre filler and hemp rope, and PP jute-fibre filler and hemp rope out-perform PVC filler rods.

When filling, nylon yarn (ballistic yarn) and Kevlar are usually used inside the conductor or during stranding; cotton yarn, PP jute-fibre filler, hemp rope and PVC filler rods are usually used as filling material during stranding.

■Separation/Bedding Layer (General)

A. Separation materials commonly include aluminium foil, PET tape, foamed PP tape, non-woven fabric and the like, used mainly in the bundling/cabling stage.

B. Because foamed PP tape and non-woven fabric are thicker and softer, they provide better flex resistance than other separation materials. However, being thicker and more costly, they are generally used in cables with electrical performance requirements or where the overall OD can accommodate them.

C. Isolation tapes are generally applied either longitudinally or helically, among which helical taping offers better flex resistance than longitudinal taping. The final choice, however, depends on the cable construction.

■Jacket (Primary)

The jacket directly protects the entire inner structure of the cable. As a general rule, a thicker jacket is better than a thinner one, a softer material better than a harder one, and a loose (tube-type) extrusion better than a tight (pressure-type) extrusion.

Jackets are mostly PVC, although TPU, TPE and other materials are also used. Since TPE and TPU offer better elongation and elasticity than PVC, they also provide better flexing performance.
