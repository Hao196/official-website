Future Development Trends of the Cable Industry

The tensions between Russia and Ukraine have triggered an energy crisis that is now spreading across Europe and the United States. Global economic indicators are continuing to decline. Since February 2022, buyer inventories in Europe and America have gradually returned to their pre-COVID levels.

Over the past two years, China appeared to be a major winner of the COVID-19 pandemic, and the country's manufacturing sector reaped rich rewards. That boom is now a thing of the past.

For one thing, sporadic outbreaks of the epidemic are still occurring across the country, causing breaks in the supply chain, and some companies in certain regions have had to cut back or suspend production.

For another, major smartphone makers such as Apple and Samsung have sharply reduced their orders, cutting demand for the associated accessory cables. Many small and medium-sized cable factories are seeing their order books shrink continuously and now face a major test of survival. Raw material prices in the wire and cable industry are also rising rapidly, with copper prices approaching the historic threshold of RMB 80,000 per tonne.

In the face of the market downturn, an elimination round has quietly begun in the wire and cable industry. Whether you join it willingly or not, you have to take part. The first to be eliminated will be labour-intensive cable processing lines and companies without any technical barriers. The industry must therefore upgrade its technology so as to stand out in this elimination round.
