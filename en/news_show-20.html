How High-Frequency Cables Attenuate

High-frequency data cables are mainly used to transmit data signals. Diffraction, obstruction and atmospheric absorption, however, are all frequency-dependent: the higher the frequency, the more readily the signal is blocked and absorbed. High-frequency signals therefore tend to suffer a drop in signal strength (voltage) caused by the resistance of the base material, while the aluminium foil layer protects and shields the information being transmitted, reducing interference from extraneous external signals on the transmission.

The main wrapping material, aluminium-foil Mylar, is a laminated aluminium-plastic composite foil that exploits the airtightness and shielding properties of aluminium foil and serves as the cable shield. The taping machine assembles the two insulated cores and the drain wire, then wraps the bundle with a layer of aluminium-foil Mylar and a layer of self-adhesive polyester tape, providing shielding for the pair and stabilising the wrapped core structure.

When analysing transmission line losses, attention should also be paid to the skin effect, the proximity effect, surface roughness, the complex permittivity of the dielectric, dielectric loss, and the frequency-dependent characteristics of impedance and time delay. The conductor's own losses are the principal component of high-frequency attenuation: they arise mainly from the resistance of the conductor itself, and under an AC signal the impedance of the conductor varies with frequency. The surface of the trace always has a certain degree of roughness, and when the wavelength of the signal approaches the scale of this roughness, resistive losses are aggravated. Moreover, because of the skin effect, high-frequency current concentrates on the surface of the conductor, further increasing the impedance losses of the conductor. Low cable attenuation can be attributed to the following factors:

a. A large centre conductor diameter (d), or a large diameter of the dielectric insulating material.
The dielectric material must be able to retain the high-frequency energy and prevent it from dissipating through the resistive components.
The lower the dissipation factor of the dielectric material, the higher its ability to transmit high-frequency energy.

b. Low resistance of the centre conductor or its coating.

c. A low dielectric constant.

d. A low skin-effect depth.
