Basic Performance of Wire and Cable

High-Temperature Resistance

Under high-temperature conditions, the deformation and ageing of the wire or cable is slowed. Conventional wires and cables are rated for continuous working temperatures of around 70-90 °C. The newer 105 °C and 125 °C wire products made from upgraded materials can easily cope with heavy household loads or conditions such as temporary short circuits.

Ageing Resistance

Under mechanical, electrical and thermal stresses and various other external factors, wires and cables must retain their original performance, resisting ageing so as to prolong service life. All wires and cables offer some ageing resistance, but the better the quality, the slower the ageing and the longer the service life.

Corrosion Resistance

Cables must resist electrochemical corrosion, biological attack, chemical agents (oils, acids, alkalis, chemical solvents, etc.), salt spray, sunlight and low temperatures, and must also offer anti-fungal and moisture-proof properties. In service, wires and cables inevitably come into contact with air, water, acidic substances and the like. Corrosion resistance limits the rate and extent of the corrosion and weathering caused by such exposure, while also slowing ageing and extending service life.

Flame Retardance

This property means that, in the event of an electrical fire, the wire or cable does not readily ignite; and if it does continue to burn, the spread of flame along the cable is greatly reduced. Once removed from the source of the fire, it extinguishes on its own.

Some electrical fires in China start when a short circuit ignites a section of wire or cable, after which the fire travels continuously along the cable run and develops into a major blaze. Flame-retardant wires and cables minimise this risk by preventing the spread of flame.

Fire Resistance

The difference between fire-resistant and flame-retardant wires and cables is that a fire-resistant cable continues to operate safely for a specified period when exposed to flame, maintaining the integrity of the circuit. Such cables are mainly used in high-rise buildings, underground railways, underground shopping streets, large power stations and important industrial and mining enterprises — wherever fire safety and fire-fighting and evacuation functions are essential.
