Operation, Maintenance and Care of Cable Extruders

The installation, adjustment and test running of the machine are generally specified in the instruction manual of the extruder. The operating method, maintenance and care of the extruder are briefly described below.

I. Operating Method of the Extruder

Operators must be thoroughly familiar with the structural characteristics of the extruder they operate, especially the structural characteristics of the screw, the characteristics of the heating and cooling control instruments, and the characteristics and assembly of the die head, so that they can correctly establish the extrusion process conditions and operate the machine properly.

The operating methods for extruding different plastic products differ, but there are also points in common. The steps common to the extrusion of all kinds of products and the precautions to be observed during operation are briefly introduced below.

1. Preparations before starting

(1) Plastics used for extrusion should meet the required drying requirements, and further drying should be carried out where necessary. Screen the raw material to remove lumps, agglomerates and mechanical impurities.

(2) Check that the water, power and air systems of the equipment are normal, and ensure that the water and air lines are unobstructed and leak-free, the electrical system is normal, and the heating system, temperature controls and all instruments operate reliably. Run the auxiliary machines at low speed with no load to observe whether the equipment operates normally. Start the vacuum pump of the sizing table and check that it operates normally. Add lubricant to all lubrication points of the equipment. Rectify any fault as soon as it is found.

(3) Mount the die head and sizing sleeve. Select the appropriate die head specification according to the type and size of the product. Assemble the die head in the following order.

1. The die head should be assembled as a whole unit and mounted on the extruder.
2. Before assembling the die head, wipe off the grease applied for storage, carefully check whether the surfaces of the mould cavity are dented, scratched or rusted, polish them where necessary, and then apply a layer of silicone oil on the flow channel surfaces.
3. Assemble the plates of the die head in order, apply high-temperature grease to the threads of the bolts, and then tighten the bolts and flanges.
4. Place the breaker plate between the die-head flanges so that it is securely held and no material leaks.
5. Before tightening the fastening bolts of the die head and the extruder connecting flange, level the die. A level can be used to level a square die head, while a round die head should be levelled against the rubber base of the sizing tool using the base of the die opening.
6. Tighten the connecting flange bolts and the die-head fastening bolts, and install the heating bands and thermocouples. Make sure the heating bands fit tightly against the outer surface of the die head.
7. Install and position the sizing sleeve, and check that the centre lines of the main machine, the sizing sleeve and the haul-off machine are aligned. After adjustment, tighten the fixing bolts. Connect the water pipes and vacuum tubes of the sizing sleeve.
8. Switch on the heating power and heat the die head and barrel evenly. At the same time, open the cooling water at the bottom of the hopper and at the gearbox, and the water inlet valve of the exhausting vacuum pump. During heating, set the temperature of each zone to 140 °C first. When the temperature reaches 140 °C, hold it for 30-40 minutes, and then raise the temperature to that of normal production. After the temperature reaches the normal production temperature, hold it for about 10 minutes so that the temperatures of all parts of the machine become stable before starting production. The holding time varies with the model of the extruder and the type of plastic material. The material should be soaked (held at temperature) for a period so that the inside and outside temperatures of the machine are consistent; otherwise, when the indicating instrument has reached the required temperature but the actual temperature is still on the low side, charging material into the extruder at the too-low actual temperature will make the melt viscosity too high, causing overload of the axial force and damage to the machine.
9. Feed the raw material for starting into the hopper, ready for use.

2. Starting

(1) The machine can be started after the temperature has been kept constant. Before starting, retighten the flange bolts of the die head and the extruder to eliminate the difference between the thermal expansion of the bolts and the die head. Tighten the die-head bolts diagonally, exerting even force. When tightening the flange nuts of the die head, the tightness around the whole circumference must be even; otherwise material will leak.

(2) To start the machine, first press the Ready for Start button, then the Start button. Then slowly rotate the screw speed adjustment knob to start the screw at low speed, and gradually increase the speed while adding material a little at a time. While feeding material, pay close attention to the main motor ammeter and the indications of the various indicating gauges. The screw torque must not exceed the red mark (generally 65%-75% of the torque gauge). Before the plastic profile is extruded, no one may stand directly in front of the die opening, to prevent injury accidents caused by bolt breakage or by the material bubbling owing to dampness. Once the plastic is extruded from the die opening, the extrudate should be cooled slowly and threaded onto the haul-off device and into the sizing die, and these devices started. Then, according to the readings of the control instruments and the requirements for the extruded product, adjust the various parts accordingly so that the whole extrusion operation reaches a normal state. Add sufficient material as required. For twin-screw extruders, use a metering feeder to feed material uniformly and at a constant rate.

(3) When the material is issuing evenly from the die and is well plastified, the extrudate can be drawn into the sizing sleeve. The degree of plastification is judged by experience, generally from the appearance of the extrudate: the surface should be glossy, free of impurities, without foaming, scorched material or discolouration; when the extrudate is squeezed thin by hand it should show no burrs or cracks and should have a certain elasticity — this indicates good plastification. If plastification is poor, the screw speed and the barrel and die-head temperatures may be adjusted as required until the requirement is met.

(4) During the extrusion process, the various process parameters should be checked regularly according to the process requirements to confirm that they are normal, and the process record sheet should be completed. Inspect the quality of the profile products against the quality inspection standard, and take corrective measures as soon as any problem is found.

3. Stopping

(1) Stop feeding, extrude all the plastic remaining in the extruder until the screw is exposed, and then switch off the power to the barrel and die head to stop heating.

(2) Switch off the power to the extruder and the auxiliary machines so that the screw and the auxiliaries stop running.

(3) Open the connecting flange of the die head and remove the die head. Clean the breaker plate and the various parts of the die head. To prevent damage to the inner surfaces of the die head during cleaning, the residual material in the die head should be removed with steel strips and blades, and the plastic adhering inside the die head should then be ground off with sandpaper, polished, and coated with machine oil or silicone oil to prevent rust.

(4) Cleaning of the screw and barrel: after removing the die head, restart the main machine and feed stopping material (or regrind) to clean the screw and barrel, running the screw at low speed (around a few tens of r/min) to reduce wear. After the stopping material is fully extruded and reduced to powder, use compressed air to blow the residual pellets and powder out through the feed opening and the vent opening repeatedly, until it is confirmed that no residue remains in the barrel. Then reduce the screw speed to zero, stop the extruder, and switch off the main power and the main cooling water valve.

(5) The safety items to be noted during extrusion include electricity, heat, rotating mechanical parts and the handling of heavy parts. The extrusion workshop must be equipped with lifting equipment for the assembly and disassembly of the die head, screw and other heavy parts, to ensure safe production.

II. Maintenance and Care of the Extruder

The screw extrusion system is maintained and serviced in two ways: routine maintenance and periodic maintenance.

(1) Routine maintenance is an ongoing routine task that does not occupy equipment operating hours and is normally completed during running. The focus is on cleaning the machine, lubricating the moving parts, tightening threaded parts that tend to work loose, and promptly checking and adjusting the motors, control instruments, working parts and pipes.

(2) Periodic maintenance is generally carried out after the extruder has run continuously for 2,500-5,000 hours, when the machine is stopped and dismantled for inspection, measurement and assessment of the wear of the main parts; parts that have worn to the specified limit should be replaced, and damaged parts repaired.

(3) Do not run the machine idle, to avoid scoring of the screw and barrel.

(4) If any abnormal sound occurs while the extruder is running, stop immediately for inspection or repair.

(5) Strictly prevent metal or other foreign matter from falling into the hopper, to avoid damaging the screw and barrel. To prevent ferrous foreign matter from entering the barrel, a magnetic catcher or magnetic frame may be installed at the feed opening where the material enters the barrel; to prevent foreign matter from falling in, the material must be screened beforehand.

(6) Keep the production environment clean; do not let rubbish or impurities mix into the material and block the filter/breaker plate, which would affect the output and quality of the product and increase the resistance of the die head.

(7) When the extruder is to be out of service for a long period, apply anti-rust grease to the working surfaces of the screw, barrel, die head and other parts. Small screws should be suspended in the air or placed in a dedicated wooden case, padded level with wooden blocks, to avoid deformation or damage.

(8) Calibrate the temperature control instruments regularly and check the correctness of their adjustment and the sensitivity of their control.

(9) The gearbox of the extruder is maintained in the same way as a standard reduction gearbox, mainly by checking the gears, bearings and other parts for wear and failure. The gearbox should use the lubricating oil specified in the machine manual, filled to the specified oil level. Too little oil results in insufficient lubrication and shortens the service life of the parts; too much oil generates excessive heat, consumes more energy and makes the oil deteriorate easily, likewise causing lubrication failure and damage to the parts. Seals (gaskets or rings) at any leaking points of the gearbox should be replaced promptly to ensure the correct lubricating oil quantity.

(10) The inner walls of the cooling water pipes attached to the extruder tend to scale, and the outer surfaces tend to corrode and rust. Careful inspection should be carried out during maintenance: excessive scale blocks the pipes and prevents effective cooling, while severe corrosion causes water leakage. Descaling and anti-corrosion measures must therefore be taken during maintenance.

(11) For the DC motor that drives the screw, pay special attention to the wear and contact condition of the brushes. The insulation resistance of the motor should also be measured regularly to confirm that it is above the specified value. In addition, check whether the connecting wires and other parts are rusted, and take protective measures.

(12) Assign a specific person to be responsible for equipment maintenance and care, and record every maintenance and repair in detail in the factory equipment management file.
